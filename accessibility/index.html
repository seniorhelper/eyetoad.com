Our Commitment to Accessibility

Eye To Ad Media ("we," "us," or "our") is dedicated to ensuring that our website, eyetoad.com, is accessible to all individuals, including those with disabilities. We believe that every person deserves equal access to information and digital services, and we are actively working to meet and exceed applicable accessibility standards.

We strive to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level AA, as published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). These guidelines explain how to make web content more accessible to people with disabilities, including users who rely on screen readers, keyboard navigation, voice control, or other assistive technologies.

In plain language

We are claiming partial conformance with WCAG 2.1 AA and an honest, ongoing effort — not perfection. Where we fall short, we say so below in Known Limitations, and we will fix it or provide the content another way if you ask.

Accessibility Standards We Follow

Our accessibility efforts are guided by the following standards and legal frameworks:

  • 📋

    WCAG 2.1 AA

    Web Content Accessibility Guidelines published by the W3C — the internationally recognized standard for digital accessibility.

  • 🏛️

    ADA Title III

    Americans with Disabilities Act requirements for places of public accommodation, increasingly applied to websites and digital services.

  • ♿

    Section 508

    Federal accessibility requirements for electronic and information technology, used here as a best-practice benchmark.

  • 🌐

    WAI-ARIA

    Authoring practices for rich internet applications, ensuring assistive technology can interpret interactive components.

Accessibility Features on This Website

We have implemented the following features and design practices:

  • Keyboard accessibility: All interactive elements — navigation links, buttons, accordions, forms and the chat assistant — are fully operable via keyboard (Tab, Enter, Space, Escape and arrow keys). The chat panel can always be closed with Escape.
  • Screen reader support: Pages use semantic HTML5 elements, ARIA landmark roles, and descriptive labels to provide a logical reading experience.
  • Focus indicators: A visible, high-contrast focus outline is applied to every interactive element for keyboard users.
  • Text resizing: Our layout adapts when browser text size is increased up to 200% without loss of content or functionality.
  • Color independence: Information is never conveyed by color alone; status indicators on this page, for example, carry a text label as well as a colored dot.
  • Contrast ratios: Text-to-background contrast meets or exceeds WCAG 2.1 AA requirements.
  • Descriptive link text: Link text describes the destination or action, avoiding generic phrases like "click here."
  • Page titles: Every page has a unique, descriptive <title> element.
  • Language attribute: The lang="en" attribute is set on every page to assist screen readers with pronunciation.
  • No hidden scrolling regions: Where content is too wide for a narrow screen, it wraps onto another line rather than scrolling sideways behind an invisible edge. Wide data tables are the one exception, and those carry a visible swipe label.
  • Reduced motion: If your operating system requests reduced motion, animated elements — including the scrolling announcement bar, the chat assistant's animations, and background video — are disabled automatically.
  • No auto-playing audio: We never auto-play sound. Our homepage uses a decorative background video that is muted, carries no audio track, is hidden from assistive technology, and does not load at all when reduced motion or data-saver mode is enabled.

Known Limitations

While we strive for full WCAG 2.1 AA conformance, we acknowledge that some areas of our website may not yet fully meet all guidelines. Known or potential limitations include:

  • Third-party tools: Some embedded tools or widgets are provided by third-party vendors and may not fully conform to WCAG 2.1 AA. We work with vendors to encourage improvements and replace tools that cannot be made accessible.
  • Legacy content: Older blog posts may contain elements that predate our current standards. We prioritize updating high-traffic pages first.
  • PDF documents: Some downloadable documents may not be fully accessible. Contact us and we will provide the content in an accessible format.
  • Third-party maps and embeds: Where we embed external mapping or scheduling services, their internal accessibility is controlled by the vendor. A phone alternative is always provided.
  • Interactive games: Our Ad Lab pages include canvas-based interactive experiences. Canvas content is difficult to expose to screen readers, and drag-based interactions cannot be operated by keyboard alone. Where a game gates something of value, we provide a non-drag alternative — but the games themselves are entertainment, not information, and nothing on the site requires playing one.

We are actively working to remediate these issues and appreciate your patience as we continue improving.

Assistive Technology Compatibility

This website is designed to be compatible with the following assistive technologies and user agents:

  • Screen readers: JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver (macOS and iOS), TalkBack (Android)
  • Browsers: Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge — current and prior major version
  • Voice control: Dragon NaturallySpeaking, Voice Control (macOS and iOS)
  • Magnification: ZoomText, Windows Magnifier, and browser zoom up to 200%
  • Alternative input: Switch access and other alternative input devices
