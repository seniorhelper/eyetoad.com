What is different about a store

The money isn't on the homepage. It's on the category page.

A service business ranks a handful of pages. A store ranks hundreds — and the ones that make money are the categories a shopper types ("walk-in tubs," "barnwood dining table") and the products they compare before buying. Most store SEO never gets past the homepage and a blog nobody reads.

Category pages that rank

Collections built around how people actually search — with real copy, not a grid of thumbnails — and an internal-link structure that tells Google which categories matter. This is where organic store revenue lives.

Product pages that answer

Dimensions, materials, fit, shipping, returns, "will this work for me" — answered on the page, in text a shopper and a machine can read. Reviews, Product and Offer schema, and photos that match the words.

Technical cleanup at scale

Faceted navigation spraying thousands of duplicate URLs, out-of-stock pages returning 200s, slow templates, thin variants — the things that quietly cap a store's rankings. Fixed at the template level, once.
