The whole job, in one picture

We code websites that do three jobs at once

Most sites are built for one of these and accidentally sabotage the other two. A beautiful site nobody can find. A site that ranks and converts nobody. A site an AI assistant cannot read a single fact from. All three have to be true at the same time, and they are decided at build time, not bolted on afterwards.

Built to convert

Every page has one obvious next action, proof placed where the doubt is, and a phone number you never have to hunt for. UX that makes it easier to do business with you.

Built to be cited by AI

Clean markup and real structured data, so assistants answering "who should I hire for this" can actually read, verify and name your business.

Built to be found by Google

Fast, crawlable, correctly structured, and organized around what people search for rather than around your internal org chart.

The real problem

Most websites are brochures with a URL

Your site lists what you do, shows a photo of the building, and waits patiently for somebody who had already decided to call you. It has never sold anyone anything.

A brochure sitting in a drawer costs you nothing but the printing. A brochure sitting on the internet costs you every visitor who arrived ready to buy and left because nothing on the page helped them do it. That is not a design problem or a copy problem. It is a job description problem: nobody ever told the website what it was for.

24/7

Proactive, not passive

A site should be working the shift you are not

Your phone stops being answered at six. Your website does not stop being visited. Somebody is comparing three businesses at 11pm on a Sunday, and whichever one made it easiest to take the next step right then is the one that gets the job on Monday.

That is the difference between a page that describes your business and a page that works for it: quote tools that answer the money question at midnight, booking that does not require a human on the other end, content that keeps earning citations while you sleep, and forms that reach you before the lead has cooled.

The walk-through

What a site built to sell actually does, step by step

Five stages. A brochure site handles the first one badly and the other four not at all.

1

They find you — or they find somebody else

A search result, an AI assistant naming three companies, or a referral checking you out before they call. All three end at your site, and all three are decided by whether your pages are fast, readable and structured. This is where most of the invisible losses happen, because you never see the person who did not arrive.

2

They decide in about five seconds whether you are serious

Not consciously, and not fairly. A slow load, a stock photo of a handshake, a 2014 layout on a phone — the judgment is made before a word is read. You do not get to argue with it. You only get to not trigger it.

3

They look for the answer to the thing they are actually worried about

Usually price, sometimes whether you handle their specific situation, occasionally whether you are still in business. If the page makes them guess, they leave and go guess on a competitor's site instead. This is where a quote tool, an honest range or a real FAQ earns its cost several times over.

4

They act — if acting is easy

One obvious next step, a form short enough to finish standing up, a tappable number, a booking option that works at midnight. Every extra field is a percentage of people who stop. Every "contact us for more information" is a person who did not.

5

You reach them before the interest cools

The lead lands where you will actually see it, with the context you need to respond usefully. A form that sends to an inbox nobody checks is the same as no form at all, and we have found that exact situation on more inherited sites than we would like to admit.

If you read those five and recognized your own site failing at three of them, you already know what this call is about. Pairing the build with conversion optimization and an SEO system is what turns the site from a cost into the thing that pays for the rest of your marketing.

So what exactly are you waiting for?

Under-capitalization is one of the most reliable ways a good business dies — not because the owner was bad at the work, but because revenue never arrived fast enough to outrun the costs. Marketing is one of the few line items that exists specifically to bring money in rather than send it out.

Every month the decision is deferred is a month of compounding you do not get back. Rankings build slowly. Reviews accumulate slowly. Citations in AI answers accumulate slowly. The business that started eighteen months ago is not smarter than you. It is just eighteen months ahead, and that gap widens on its own.

Straight answers

Custom website design questions, answered honestly

Custom website design means the layout, the code and the content structure are built for one specific business rather than adapted from a template that thousands of other companies are also using. In practice the difference shows up in three places: the site is faster because nothing is loaded that the page does not need, it is structured around the actions you want a visitor to take instead of around what a theme happened to offer, and it can do things a template cannot, such as a quoting tool or a booking flow built for the way you actually sell.

A template is a general-purpose shell that has to support every kind of business, so it ships with code for features you will never use, and that weight is carried by every visitor on every page load. A custom coded website contains only what your business needs. The visible result is usually speed and clarity. The invisible result is that you are not fighting the theme every time you want to change something, and you are not one plugin update away from a broken page.
