What it means for marketing in Lakewood

  • More than half of Lakewood's homes (56.8%) were built before 1980, against 40.6% statewide. Repair, replacement and remodeling searches (furnaces, sewer lines, windows, roofs) make up a bigger share of local demand here than in newer suburbs.
  • Only 58.1% of Lakewood homes are owner-occupied, compared with 66.3% across Colorado, and median gross rent is $1,746. A Lakewood campaign should speak to renters and property managers as well as homeowners, because both groups search for the same services.
  • Median household income is $85,789, below the Colorado median of $92,470, while the median home value is $548,200. Clear pricing signals and reviews that mention value carry weight here, in a city that has held steady at about 156,868 people (2024 estimate).

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2019–2023 5-year estimates (tables B19013, B25077, B25003, B25034, B15003, B08303) and Vintage 2024 population estimates.

Local context

  • Lakewood is a home-rule city: the 3% city share of sales tax is remitted directly to the City of Lakewood rather than the state, and Belmar businesses use a different combined rate (5.5%) than most of the city (7.5%). City of Lakewood Finance Department
  • The Colorado SBDC's Denver Metro center is hosted by Red Rocks Community College at its Lakewood campus, 13300 W. 6th Avenue, offering free advising to small businesses. Colorado Small Business Development Center
  • Lakewood covers about 43.5 square miles of land, large enough that Google Maps results change noticeably between Belmar, Green Mountain and the south end. U.S. Census Bureau, 2023 Gazetteer Files
  • Lakewood's Office of Economic Development publishes a dashboard on federal government cuts, a local issue because the Denver Federal Center sits inside the city. City of Lakewood Office of Economic Development
