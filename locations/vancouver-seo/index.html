How the Lower Mainland searches

Metro Vancouver is a federation of 21 municipalities, one electoral area and one treaty First Nation, home to roughly 2.6 million people at the 2021 census. Locals rely on Google Canada and Maps, and they care a great deal about which side of which bridge you are on.

Languages differ by city

In Surrey, Punjabi is the mother tongue of more than one in five residents. In Richmond, Cantonese and Mandarin each account for over a fifth. A plan that suits Kitsilano will not suit Newton or No. 3 Road without proper pages in those languages.

The wet season is the season

Roughly October to April, rain drives roofing, gutter, drainage, mould and restoration searches, while dry summers bring painting, decks, landscaping and heat-pump installs. Pages need to exist before the first atmospheric river, not after it.

Strata and high costs

Many homes are strata units, and prices are among the highest in Canada, so buyers compare carefully and strata councils often collect several quotes. Clear licensing, insurance and warranty details on the page carry weight.

Beyond Google, Vancouver home-service buyers check HomeStars and Yelp, restaurants live or die on Google and Yelp reviews, and YellowPages.ca still feeds citation data. Bridge and tunnel traffic matters too: a Richmond business that does not want to cross the Oak Street Bridge in rush hour should say which areas it covers, plainly, so neither Google nor customers are confused.
