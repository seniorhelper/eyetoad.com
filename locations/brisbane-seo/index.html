The Queensland search calendar

People in Brisbane use google.com.au and Maps much like other Australians, but their reasons for searching are distinctly Queensland: heat, storms, newcomers, and a long build-up to the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games.

A region still filling up

South East Queensland has been growing faster than the national average, with forecasts of roughly 4.5 million residents around the time of the 2032 Games. Every new household needs a GP, a mechanic, an electrician and a school, and builds that list mostly online.

Storms, floods and heat

From October the storm season brings hail, flash flooding and roof damage, and summer heat pushes air-conditioning and pool searches. Roofers, restorers, arborists and sparkies who publish early are already on the map when the weather turns.

Infrastructure moving demand

Cross River Rail, with new underground stations at Boggo Road, Woolloongabba, Albert Street and Roma Street, plus the Games venue program, is changing where people work and live. Businesses near those precincts should mention them on the page.

Queensland also skips daylight saving, so Brisbane sits an hour behind Sydney and Melbourne for half the year. That small detail matters for the opening hours on your profile and for booking pages that serve customers over the border in northern New South Wales, where clocks do change.
