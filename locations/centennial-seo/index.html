📍 Centennial · Arapahoe County, Colorado

Centennial SEO for a City the Post Office Still Calls Something Else

Centennial incorporated in 2001. The USPS never updated the ZIP codes. So businesses sitting inside the city have addresses that read Englewood, Littleton or Aurora — and quietly rank for none of them properly. Fixing that is usually the single highest-return thing a Centennial business can do.

Short version

Centennial has a local SEO problem that no other Denver suburb has, and most businesses here do not know they have it. The city incorporated on 7 February 2001 — at the time the largest municipal incorporation in US history — but the postal service still assigns Aurora, Englewood and Littleton as the default city names for Centennial's ZIP codes.

So your listings say one city, your customers live in another, and Google gets conflicting signals. Meanwhile this is the wealthiest market we cover, with a median household income of about $128,167. Fix the address problem and you are competing for that in a field where most rivals have not.

The address problem

Start here, because everything else on this page is downstream of it.

Before 2001, this area was unincorporated Arapahoe County. Residents voted to incorporate on 12 September 2000 — approved by 77% of voters — and the City of Centennial was formed on 7 February 2001 from the former Castlewood and Southglenn census-designated places.

It was the largest municipal incorporation in United States history. And the postal service, reasonably enough, did not redraw its ZIP code place names to match.

Twenty-five years later, it still has not. So a business physically inside Centennial commonly has a mailing address that reads Englewood, Littleton or Aurora.

How disconnected these designations are

Centennial and Englewood do not share a boundary at all — yet Englewood appears on Centennial mailing addresses. Meanwhile some parts of Centennial are entirely surrounded by Aurora, and vice versa. The city's boundaries are genuinely irregular, spanning east to west across I-25. The postal map and the actual map are two different things.

Now think about what that does to local search.

1. Your listings may not say Centennial at all

If your Google Business Profile, your website footer and your directory listings all carry the postal city, you are giving Google a consistent signal that you are an Englewood business. Someone searching "plumber in Centennial" may never see you — despite you being three miles from them, inside the city.

2. Directories disagree with each other

Some data aggregators use the municipal boundary. Others use the postal designation. So the same business ends up listed as Centennial in one place and Littleton in another.

Local rankings depend heavily on your name, address and phone being identical everywhere. This is the exact inconsistency that suppresses them — and in Centennial it happens by default rather than by mistake.

3. Your customers search using the wrong city too

This is the part businesses miss. A resident whose mail says Littleton frequently searches "dentist Littleton" — even though they live in Centennial. So optimising purely for "Centennial" misses a real share of your own neighbours.

What to actually do about it

The instinct is to pick one name and commit. That is the wrong move — you lose whichever half of the demand you did not choose.

The correct approach is to be findable under both, with each name doing the job it is suited to.

  • Formal address fields: use the postal city. Deliverability and Google verification depend on it, and fighting the USPS designation causes more problems than it solves.
  • Google Business Profile description and service area: make Centennial explicit. This is where you tell Google what the address does not.
  • On-page content and title tags: Centennial, named plainly, on pages that genuinely serve it.
  • Schema markup: declare the city and service area properly so machines can resolve the ambiguity your address creates.
  • Reviews: when customers naturally mention Centennial in review text, that is a strong corroborating signal — and it is free.
  • Citations: audit for the mismatch and reconcile deliberately rather than letting each directory guess.

A median household income around $128,167 puts Centennial well ahead of Thornton at $103,088 and Westminster at $100,272. And the median age of about 41.8 is meaningfully older, which matters more than it sounds.

Older, wealthier households own more property, have more complex needs, and are considerably less price-sensitive. They replace rather than repair. They hire specialists rather than generalists. The city is also served by Cherry Creek School District across most of its area, with Littleton Public Schools covering western portions — both well regarded, which sustains property values and keeps families in place.

The Tech Center dimension

Centennial sits close to the Denver Tech Center and the Greenwood Village office corridor, which gives it something residential suburbs lack: genuine business-to-business demand next door. IT support, commercial services, office services, professional advice and business-focused trades all have a dense concentration of potential clients within a short drive. Daytime search behaviour in the north of the city looks noticeably different from the residential south because of it.

The industries that do best in Centennial

The pattern is straightforward: high ticket, considered purchase, quality over price.

Premium home services

Property values and household income support the top end of every trade. Roof replacement rather than patching. Full HVAC systems rather than repairs. Kitchen and bath renovation, landscape design, custom fencing, finished basements.

The buyer here will pay for someone who explains the options properly. Competing on being cheapest actively hurts you in this market.

Healthcare, dental and aesthetics

A median age of 41.8 with $128,167 median income is close to ideal for elective and specialist care. Orthodontics, cosmetic dentistry, dermatology, med spa services, physical therapy, vision and hearing all perform strongly.

Professional and financial services

Established households with real assets need estate planning, tax work, financial advice and legal counsel. Combine that with the Tech Center proximity and you have both consumer and business demand in the same place.

Business-to-business services

This is Centennial's genuine differentiator. Few residential suburbs sit this close to a major office corridor. IT support, commercial cleaning, managed services, commercial landscaping, print and signage, corporate catering — all have a dense client base within a short drive, and very few compete properly on local search.

The category most under-served here

Business-to-business services, consistently. Most B2B firms near the Tech Center rely on referrals and outbound, and almost none have done serious local SEO. Meanwhile decision-makers absolutely do search — for IT support, for a commercial plumber, for a signage company. It is one of the quietest open fields in the entire metro.

Centennial's neighbours matter more than usual here

Centennial borders Littleton to the west, Greenwood Village and Cherry Hills Village to the north, Aurora to the north and east, and Lone Tree, Parker and Foxfield to the south and south-east.

Because the boundaries are so irregular — and because three of those neighbours appear on Centennial mailing addresses — the practical service area of a Centennial business almost always spans several cities whether they planned it or not.

That is an opportunity if you structure it deliberately. It is a problem if your service area is a vague radius and your listings cannot agree which city you are in.

Littleton SEO →West, and one of your postal names. Older, better-known, deeper competition. Aurora SEO →North and east, and another postal name. Much larger market, much harder. Centennial vs Littleton, since people ask

They are adjacent and constantly confused — partly because Littleton is one of the postal names for Centennial ZIPs. Littleton is older, has a defined historic downtown and stronger name recognition, so search volume is higher and competition deeper. Centennial has higher household income and less well-optimised competitors. If you serve both, build genuine signals for each rather than assuming one campaign covers it.
