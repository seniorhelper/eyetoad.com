What clients actually do

They don't admire the site. They interrogate it.

A person with a legal problem opens three firms in three tabs and reads the way a juror reads: who are you, have you done this before, what will it cost me, can I reach you. The firm whose site answers first, in plain language, gets the call. The one with the drone footage of the skyline gets the second tab closed.

Practice-area pages that answer

Not "we handle personal injury" — what happens after a crash, what the firm does first, how fees work, what a client should bring. Question-shaped, direct, and structured so Google and AI systems can quote them.

Attorneys as verifiable entities

Bar admissions, credentials, matter types, a real photo, and schema that says the same — so a client and a machine can confirm the person is who the page says. Firms that hide their lawyers lose to firms that show them.

Intake that works at 11 p.m.

A phone number in text, a short consultation request, an assistant that answers the first question and captures the lead inside bar rules, and follow-up in minutes — because the second firm in the tab is one click away.

How we build it

Answer. Verify. Convert.

We start with the practice areas that carry the firm and the questions clients ask before calling. Each becomes a page written by people who have sold high-stakes services — reviewed against bar advertising rules and flagged for your counsel. Attorneys get entity pages. Results and reviews get placed honestly next to the practice they relate to.

Then the intake path, built for a phone, with a sales-trained assistant that respects the line between information and advice, and follow-up measured in minutes. And the search layer throughout: speed, LegalService and Attorney schema, direct answers, city structure for the markets you serve, and facts consistent across the site, the Business Profile and the legal directories so AI answers can cite the firm.
