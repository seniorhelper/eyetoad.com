What is restaurant SEO?

Restaurant SEO is the work of being findable and convincing on the surfaces where people decide where to eat — Google Search, Google Maps, Apple Maps, review platforms, and now AI assistants. In practice it means claiming your listings, publishing your menu as readable text rather than a picture, earning steady recent reviews, keeping your details identical everywhere, and running a site that loads fast on a phone. It is not a trick played on an algorithm. It is publishing accurate facts where machines and diners can both read them. We wrote the long version as a free guide: the complete restaurant SEO guide, about 35 minutes, nothing gated.

The shift, dated

Discovery and booking collapsed into one step

For a year, assistants could find your restaurant and then politely give up. That gap closed in August 2026, and it changes what your marketing is actually for.

What changed

The assistant finishes the job

A diner describes what they want in ordinary language — neighborhood, time, party size, budget, dietary needs, atmosphere — sees real availability in the answer, and books. Reservations run through OpenTable, Resy and Yelp. Google and Copilot have their own equivalents.

Why it stings

Your site lost its second chance

Before this, an assistant could recommend you and the diner still had to visit a website or a map, which gave you one more chance to convince them. Now the decision can complete inside the conversation. Being named in the recommendation is no longer a step toward the sale — increasingly it is the sale.

The unfair part, in your favor

There is no ad slot inside the answer

You cannot buy your way into an AI recommendation. Inclusion is earned through documentation — readable facts, corroborated across sources. That is unusually fair to independents, and it is a window that closes as the category catches on.

If you take reservations only by phone, an assistant can recommend you and still not complete the booking. Deeper on the mechanism: how AI assistants decide which restaurants to name.

Field notes

Four things we find in almost every audit

None are exotic. Nearly all are free to fix, and several are being made right now by restaurants that are otherwise excellent.

Nobody can log into the profile

Set up years ago by a web designer or a former manager who has since gone quiet. The listing is live, wrong in some small way, and unfixable. Reclaiming takes days to weeks, so start before you need to.

Holiday hours never set

The most expensive small mistake in the category. Google says open, someone drives across town on a holiday, and you get a one-star review for a decision made months earlier. Set the whole year in one sitting.

A delivery app outranks you for your own name

Someone searches your restaurant by name and lands on a third-party page where you pay commission. You lose the margin and the relationship on a search you should own outright.

Crawlers blocked by accident

Some site builders block AI crawlers by default, and some sites still carry a noindex tag left from development. Both are silent, both are total, and both take two minutes to check.
