The Aesthetic Buying Journey

How Someone Actually Ends Up on Your Treatment Table

Four steps, and your website is only involved in one of them. Most med spa marketing is built for step one and then hopes for the best.

📱

1. They see a result and want it

Instagram, TikTok, a friend's face. This is where desire is created, and it is the part most practices already do well. It is also rented ground — reach depends on an algorithm you do not control.

🤖

2. They research the treatment, often with AI

What is it, what does it cost, how long does it last, does it hurt. Increasingly asked of an assistant rather than typed into Google. Practices whose content answers these questions get cited; the rest are invisible at this stage.

Treatment plus location. The map listings appear first, and the comparison happens right there — rating, review recency, photos, how maintained the listing looks. Many people never open a website at all.

A booking tool that fights a phone, a form nobody answers until tomorrow, a call that goes to voicemail mid-treatment. Aesthetic decisions are impulsive and the impulse has a short half-life.

Why Aesthetics Is Different

Med Spa SEO Is Not Local SEO With Softer Photography

Four things make aesthetic search genuinely different from ordinary local SEO, and agencies that miss them build campaigns that look lovely and never fill a Tuesday afternoon.

1. Clients search treatments, not businesses. Almost nobody types "med spa" and browses. They type the specific thing they want — a brand-name injectable, a device, a procedure — because they already decided what they want on social. That means your ranking is decided page by page, treatment by treatment. A combined services page competes against five practices who each wrote one focused page, and it loses to all five.

2. The content is regulated. Result photos are protected health information. Testimonials and endorsements fall under FTC standards. Injectables and energy-based devices are medical procedures with licensure and supervision requirements attached. This is not a reason to market timidly — it is a reason to have someone building the content who knows where the lines are before your medical director has to redline the whole site.

3. Trust is doing more work than copy is. A client is handing you her face. The signals that convert her are specific and mostly not prose: who is performing the treatment and what are their credentials, real results from real patients, reviews that mention the actual treatment, and a clear honest account of recovery. Trust architecture is conversion work, not decoration.

4. The money is in the second year. Aesthetics is a repeat business dressed as a transaction business. A practice that measures success by new clients acquired and never measures rebooking rate is optimising the wrong half of its revenue. We build for both, because acquisition without retention is the most expensive way to run an aesthetic practice.

Advertising, Privacy & Licensure

The Rules That Actually Govern Med Spa Marketing

Most marketing agencies have never read any of these, which is how practices end up with a beautiful results gallery that nobody signed a release for. In our experience the violations are almost always accidental — somebody wrote confident marketing copy without knowing it was regulated.

FTC · Advertising claims

Objective claims need substantiation before you publish them

The Federal Trade Commission requires that objective advertising claims be supported by adequate evidence at the time they are made. In aesthetics that covers efficacy statements, comparative claims about devices or providers, and anything that promises a specific outcome or timeline. "Substantiate it later if challenged" is not the standard.

Risky: "Permanent results" · "The best injector in Denver" · "Lose 30 lbs in 90 days" · a specific number of sessions presented as a guarantee.
Safer: describe what the treatment does, what is typical, and what varies by person — then let the results gallery and reviews do the persuading.
FTC · Endorsements & testimonials

Results shown must not create a misleading impression

The FTC's endorsement standards govern how testimonials and results may be presented. A key point practices routinely miss: a disclaimer such as "results not typical" does not by itself cure an otherwise misleading impression. Material connections must also be disclosed — if an endorser was compensated, given free treatment, or is a staff member or family, the audience has to be told.

Practical effect: influencer partnerships and staff testimonials need clear disclosure, and incentivised or fabricated reviews are squarely off-limits. Read the FTC's plain-language endorsement guidance and its health claims guidance.
HIPAA · Patient images & testimonials

Before-and-after photos need written authorization

Where your practice is a covered entity, recognisable patient photographs and identified testimonials are protected health information. Using them for marketing requires a valid written HIPAA authorization — a general consent-to-treat form is not the same thing, and neither is verbal permission or a comment on a social post. The authorization should be specific about where and for how long the images may be used.

Not sufficient: "She said it was fine" · a treatment consent form with no marketing clause · reposting a client's own photo without written permission.
Required: a signed, specific marketing authorization on file before publication, with a documented process for withdrawal. See HHS guidance.
State · Licensure & supervision

How you describe your team has to match what they may legally do

Injectables and many energy-based treatments are medical procedures, and states set their own requirements for who may perform them, what physician involvement or delegation is required, and how a practice may be owned and structured. These rules differ by state and have been actively legislated in recent years. Your marketing has to describe your team accurately against the rules where you operate.

Practical effect: titles, "medical director" references and provider credentials on your site should be verified against current requirements — in Colorado, with DORA's Division of Professions and Occupations and your own counsel. We will not write a credential onto a page that your practice has not confirmed.

⚠️ Read the current text, not this summary. Everything above is a plain-language description written to help you spot problems on your own website. It is not a quotation, it is not legal or medical advice, and it is not a substitute for current official guidance. Advertising standards, privacy rules and state licensure requirements are all amended, and aesthetic medicine has been an active area for regulators. Verify against the primary sources linked here, and put anything close to a line in front of your medical director and your counsel rather than your marketing agency.

Plain English

The Terms in Your Marketing Proposal, Defined

If an agency uses one of these without being able to explain it in a sentence, that is worth noticing.

Med Spa SEO

Improving an aesthetic practice's visibility in search for the treatment and location queries clients actually use — across organic results, Google Maps and AI-generated answers.

Treatment page

A landing page dedicated to a single treatment. Search engines resolve a page to one topic, so a combined services page competes poorly against practices that built one page each.

Local Pack

The map and three listings Google shows above the organic results on local searches. For most med spas it is the highest-value position on the page.

Google Business Profile

The free listing that determines your presence in Maps and the local pack. The primary category is the single most consequential field on it.

FTC Endorsement Guides

The federal standards for advertising testimonials — how results may be shown, and when a connection between practice and endorser must be disclosed.

HIPAA authorization

Written patient permission required before recognisable treatment photos or identified testimonials may be used in marketing. A consent-to-treat form is not the same thing.

Consultation conversion rate

The share of booked consultations that become paying clients. Usually a bigger lever on revenue than traffic volume, and most practices do not measure it.

Client lifetime value

Total revenue from a client across every visit rather than the first. Aesthetic treatments repeat, so this — not the first ticket — determines what you can afford to spend to acquire someone.

AIO

AI Optimization — structuring content and schema so a practice appears inside Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode answers.

GEO

Generative Engine Optimization — earning citation inside answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude through entity consistency and quotable content.

Zero-click search

Med Spa SEO in Denver — Straight Answers

No vague promises. Where we cannot guarantee something, we say so.

Med spa SEO is the practice of improving an aesthetic practice's visibility in search results for the treatment and location queries prospective clients actually use. For a Denver practice it has four working parts: a Google Business Profile strong enough to hold a position in the local pack, a dedicated page for each treatment category rather than one combined services page, a current review profile, and a booking path that works on a phone. Since 2026 it also means AI search visibility, because clients increasingly ask an assistant to explain a treatment before they search for a provider.

The Denver aesthetic market is crowded and the decision is made in search. Someone deciding where to get Botox or laser hair removal compares several practices in the map listings — on rating, review recency, photos and how complete the listing looks — often without visiting anybody's website. A practice that is not visible at that moment is not in the comparison at all, regardless of how good its results are.

For any treatment you genuinely want bookings from, yes. Search engines resolve a page to a topic. A single services page listing Botox, fillers, laser hair removal, CoolSculpting and Hydrafacial gives the engine no clear topic to resolve to, and it will lose to five competitors who each wrote one focused page. Start with your highest-margin and highest-repeat treatments rather than trying to build twenty pages at once — the planner above will estimate what your menu needs.

Result photos are among the strongest conversion assets an aesthetic practice has, and properly labelled images help search engines understand what you offer. Two sets of rules apply. First, recognisable patient photographs and identified testimonials are protected health information, so written HIPAA authorization is required before using them in marketing. Second, the FTC's endorsement standards govern how results may be presented — including that a disclaimer such as "results not typical" does not by itself cure a misleading impression. We build the content system; your medical director and counsel approve the specific consent and disclosure language.

The most valuable terms pair a treatment with a location: "Botox Denver," "lip filler Denver," "laser hair removal Denver," "CoolSculpting Denver," "Hydrafacial Denver," "medical spa near me." Underneath those sit the questions people ask before they are ready to book — how long filler lasts, what recovery is like, what a treatment costs in Denver — and those questions are where AI answers now appear. We map both layers against your actual treatment menu and current site authority.
