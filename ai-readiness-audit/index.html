Direct answer: An AI readiness audit checks whether AI assistants and agents can complete four stages with your business: find it (one consistent identity across Google, Apple, Bing, Yelp, directories and your own schema), trust it (recent reviews above 4.5, visible license and insurance, a secure site that loads, no contradictions), quote it (a published price or range it can repeat, hours and service area it can read) and book it (live availability and a booking method that is not a static form). Eye To Ad Media offers it at three levels: a free ten-second scan on our sister site Doorhanger.ai, the $297 written AI search visibility audit delivered in five business days, or $750 with the top fixes done for you and the answers re-checked 30 days later. Anything beyond the fixes is quoted, or done at $100 a task on the à la carte menu.

The four stages

Fifteen checks, four stages, one score.

Every assistant runs roughly this sequence before it recommends a business, and every booking agent runs it before it opens your calendar. We published the full checklist in the AI readiness checklist for 2026; here is what each stage covers and what failing it sounds like.

1

Found (checks 1 to 4)

One name, phone, address or service area and hours everywhere; a dedicated page per service; AI crawlers allowed in; presence on third-party lists and industry sites. Failing sounds like: the assistant names three competitors and never mentions you.

2

Trusted (checks 5 to 9)

Rating at or above 4.5 with 20 then 50 reviews, something newer than 90 days, every review answered; license, insurance and founding year in text; a secure site that loads; no contradictions between site, profile and listings. Failing sounds like: “also listed nearby, though I couldn’t confirm their hours.”

3

Quoted (checks 10 to 12)

A price or range per service the assistant can repeat; hours and area as data; the words customers use mapped to the services you name. Failing sounds like: “they don’t list pricing, so you’ll want to call. Competitor B lists repairs at $150 to $450.”

4

Booked (checks 13 to 15)

Live availability an agent can read; a booking method that is not a static form; a confirmation within a minute, day or night. Failing sounds like: “I can’t see their availability, but here’s their contact page.” Passing sounds like: “Tuesday at 8 or Wednesday at 10. Want me to book Tuesday?”

The thresholds come from the 2026 data, not from us: BrightLocal’s 2026 survey (31% of consumers require 4.5 stars, 47% skip businesses with fewer than 20 reviews, 45% used an AI tool for a local recommendation) and Whitespark’s 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors (a dedicated page per service and presence on curated lists among the top AI visibility factors). When we run the fifteen checks on Denver service businesses the median score is around 6; established companies with strong reviews often sit at 8 or 9 and are surprised.

How it works

From scan to bookable in about six weeks.

  1. Run the free scan (today)

    Ten seconds. You get a score out of 15 and the factor-by-factor fixes. For about a third of businesses that is enough to act on without buying anything, and we would rather you did.

  2. Order the audit (day 1)

    Pay $297 or $750 on the audit page. We email a short intake: your top five services, the suburbs you serve, your price book if you have one, and a secure way to share site access if you chose done for you. Never type passwords into a form.

  3. The reading (days 1 to 5)

    A person runs your customers’ questions on every assistant on the same day, records the answers, scores the checks, traces the sources and ranks the fixes. The written report arrives by day 5.

  4. The fixes (days 5 to 15, done-for-you option)

    Identity cleanup across listings, price ranges on service pages, the schema set, the credentials strip, robots.txt and the Search Console toggle. Anything outside the top fixes (new service pages, a booking endpoint, a review system) is quoted before we touch it.

  5. The re-check (day 35 to 45)

    We run the same questions again and send you the before and after. If the assistants still will not quote or book you, the report says why and what the next step costs, in plain words.

Dates count from the day we receive your intake and, for done for you, site access. If something on our side slips, we tell you before the date, not after.
