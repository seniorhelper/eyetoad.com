Press releases and SEO

Do Press Releases Still Help SEO? What They Do in 2026

By Zachary Wennstedt, founderPublished October 9, 202610 minute read

Short answer: a press release will not move your rankings through its links, and trying to make it do that breaks Google’s rules. It can still do real work for search, for AI answers and for trust. Here is what that work is.

The short version: Google treats links in press releases as advertising, and its spam policies list keyword-stuffed release links as a link scheme. Use releases for what they are good at: getting a real announcement onto pages that journalists, customers, search engines and AI assistants read, in your own words, with your facts stated plainly.

What changed, and when

For a stretch in the 2000s, press release sites were a cheap way to buy links. Write a thin release, stuff it with exact-match anchor text, push it to hundreds of syndication sites, and watch the rankings move. Google closed that door years ago. Its spam policies name “links with optimized anchor text in articles or press releases distributed on other sites” as an example of a link scheme, and its guidance on paid links asks publishers to mark them rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow".

Reputable release services now do exactly that. Every link in a release on a legitimate wire is marked sponsored or nofollow, which tells search engines not to count it as a vote. If a service promises followed links or ranking gains from links, that is a reason to walk away, not a feature.

What a release still does for search

Taking the link value off the table does not make a release useless. It changes what the release is for. Four things still matter:

  • It puts a dated, attributable fact on the web. “Opened a second location on March 1” or “now carries this product line” becomes a public statement with a date and a company name attached. Search engines and AI systems can find it, quote it and connect it to your business.
  • It can be found by people who write about your industry. Reporters, bloggers and newsletter editors watch feeds and searches for their beat. A clear, factual release is something they can pick up. Any coverage they write is the part that can earn real links and real attention.
  • It feeds branded search. When someone hears about you and searches your name, a recent release on a credible site gives them something current to find besides your own homepage.
  • It gets indexed fast. A good release site submits new pages to search engines straight away. Bing and several other engines accept instant submissions through IndexNow; Google does not participate in IndexNow, but news-style pages on well-run sites are usually crawled quickly anyway.

Why AI answers make releases more useful, not less

AI assistants answer questions by retrieving pages and quoting the parts that state facts clearly. A release written properly is exactly that kind of page: who did what, where, when, with numbers and a named source. A release that states “Northgate Plumbing opened a second Aurora service center on March 1, 2026, adding 14 technicians” gives an assistant a sentence it can repeat and attribute.

The same release written as “Northgate Plumbing is thrilled to announce an exciting new chapter” gives it nothing. The difference is not the distribution. It is whether there is a checkable fact in the first paragraph. We cover the wider picture in how to get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI and in our guide to entity signals.

What makes something worth a release

The fastest way to waste a release is to publish one about nothing. A useful test: would a stranger who follows your industry care that this happened? Things that usually pass:

  • A new location, a new service area or a new product line
  • A hire or promotion that changes what customers get
  • A real number: jobs added, customers served, a measured result
  • An event with a date, a place and a reason to attend
  • Guidance that answers a question your customers keep asking, stated plainly

Things that usually fail: a website refresh with no change for customers, an award you paid to enter, a “we are excited” announcement with no fact in it, and anything written mainly to hold a keyword.

How to write one that works

  1. Lead with the fact. The first sentence says who did what, where and when. If someone reads only that sentence, they have the news.
  2. Add one number. Specific beats impressive. “14 technicians” is more useful than “significant growth.”
  3. Quote a real person saying something useful. Not “we are thrilled,” but the reason behind the decision, in words a person would actually say.
  4. Link to the one page that matters. One link to the page a reader should act on. It will be marked sponsored, which is fine: its job is to send people, not ranking credit.
  5. Close with a short “About” paragraph. What the company does, where, and since when. This is the paragraph AI systems and reporters reuse most.

Where to publish it

Distribution matters less than it used to and honesty matters more. The things worth paying for are a permanent page on a credible site, correct labeling, feeds that other publishers can pick up, fast submission to search engines, and a record of where the release actually went. Our own service, Distribute Press Releases, is built that way: every release gets a public receipt listing each place it landed, and it routes each one to the local sites where it fits instead of blasting it everywhere.

Whoever you use, ask three questions before paying: are links marked sponsored, can you show me where my last release appeared, and what happens if a channel does not take it. A good service answers all three without hesitating.

Where releases fit in a growth plan

A release is a supporting move, not the plan. It works best on top of the basics: a site that answers questions clearly, a complete Google Business Profile, reviews that keep coming, and pages that turn visits into calls. When those are in place, a release about something real gives search engines, AI assistants and people a fresh, dated reason to mention you. When they are not, it is a page announcing news to a website that cannot convert the attention.

Questions readers ask

Do press releases help SEO?

Not through their links, which Google treats as advertising and which reputable services mark sponsored or nofollow. They can help indirectly by putting dated facts about your business on credible pages, by earning real coverage, and by giving AI assistants clear statements to quote.

Are press release links dofollow?

On reputable services, no. Google asks that paid links be marked sponsored or nofollow, and its spam policies list optimized anchor text in press releases as a link scheme.

Can a press release get my business into AI answers?

It can help when the release states clear, checkable facts in its first paragraph and is published on a page AI systems can read. Vague announcements with no facts are rarely quoted.

How often should a small business publish a press release?

Only when something real happens: a new location, product, hire, event or useful guidance. For many small businesses that is a few times a year, not every month.

What should I avoid in a press release?

Keyword-stuffed anchor text, claims you cannot back up, announcements with no fact in them, and any service that promises ranking gains from links.
