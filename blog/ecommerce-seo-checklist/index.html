The Ecommerce SEO Checklist: Seven Steps to Organic Revenue

Why store SEO is different

A service business needs one page per service and town. A store needs one page per category and product, and there can be thousands, most of them generated by a platform that was never told what a search engine needs. The result is the same on nearly every store we audit: a homepage that ranks for the brand, category pages that rank for nothing, and ad spend covering the gap.

How to use this: work the sections in order. The first three fix the leaks that stop everything else from working. Sections four to seven build the asset. If you only do one thing, do category pages.

1. Crawl and index hygiene

  • Every filter and sort combination that creates a URL is either canonicalized to the base category or blocked from indexing
  • Product variants (color, size) resolve to one canonical product URL
  • Out-of-stock products stay live with a restock or alternative message; discontinued ones redirect to the parent category
  • Sitemap lists only canonical, indexable URLs
  • Site search results pages are noindexed

Run your homepage and a category page through PageSpeed Insights. Google’s Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds and CLS under 0.1, measured at the 75th percentile of real visits.

2. Category pages that rank

The category page is where the money keyword lives, and most platforms give it a title and a grid of products and nothing else. Add 150 to 400 words of real copy above or below the grid: what the category is, who it is for, how to choose, and links to the subcategories and buying guides. Give it a title that leads with the keyword and an H1 that matches.

3. Product pages that answer

  • Unique title and description per product, never the manufacturer’s default
  • Specifications in real text: dimensions, materials, weight, compatibility
  • Shipping cost, delivery time and return policy visible on the page
  • Product schema with price, availability and real reviews
  • Images named for the product and sized for the page

These details are what get a product quoted by an AI shopping assistant. Our guide to AI SEO explains the mechanics; the short version is that assistants quote pages that state facts.

4. Internal linking

Every product links up to its category and sideways to related products. Every category links up to its parent and down to its children. Buying guides link to the exact products they recommend. Breadcrumbs everywhere, with BreadcrumbList markup. Orphan products, the ones reachable only through search or a filter, do not rank.

5. Buying guides, not blog posts

Skip the lifestyle blog. Write guides that answer the question a buyer has before choosing a category: how to choose, what size, which material, what to avoid. Each guide links to the category and to three products. Our ecommerce SEO service page describes how we scope these.

6. Reviews and trust

Collect reviews on the product page, not only on a third-party widget that Google cannot read. Reply to negative ones. Show shipping and returns before checkout. Trust signals decide whether a ranking page converts, and conversion is the point; see our conversion optimization page for the full list.

7. Measure revenue, not rankings

  • Organic revenue by landing page, monthly
  • Category page impressions and clicks in Search Console
  • Indexed page count against canonical page count (they should be close)
  • Core Web Vitals field data, quarterly

Search builds slower than ads and it keeps working after the invoice stops. That is the trade, and for most stores it is the right one.

Questions readers ask

How long does ecommerce SEO take?

Technical fixes can show within weeks. Category rankings usually build over three to nine months depending on competition. No one can promise a date.

Should I block filter pages?

Canonicalize or noindex them unless a filter combination has real search demand of its own, in which case give it a proper page.

Do product descriptions need to be unique?

Yes. Manufacturer defaults appear on hundreds of stores, and Google has no reason to rank yours over the others.

Which platform is best for SEO?

The platform matters less than the template quality and the category structure. Shopify, WooCommerce and custom stores can all rank well when the checklist is done.

Does this apply to a small store with fifty products?

Especially. A small catalog can have every product and category page done properly, which large stores rarely manage.
