Google AI Mode and Your Local Business: What Changes, What to Do

By Zachary Wennstedt, founderPublished October 2, 202613 minute read

AI Mode does not run your search. It runs five of them and writes one answer. Here is what that means for a business that lives on local search, and the five things to fix first.

What AI Mode actually does with a search

For twenty years a Google search was one query matched against one index, returning ten links. Google AI Mode breaks that model. When a person types a question into AI Mode, Google's system can split that question into several related searches, run them all at once, read the results of each, and write one answer that pulls from all of them. Google describes this as a query fan-out technique in its own documentation about AI features in Search.

The short version: your business no longer competes for one search. It competes for the handful of hidden searches an AI runs on the customer's behalf, and it gets named in the answer only if it shows up well in enough of them.

Think about what a nervous homeowner types at eleven at night: "emergency plumber near me open now, good reviews." A classic search matches those words. AI Mode treats them as a brief. Who is open at this hour? Who has recent reviews that mention responsiveness? What does after-hours work cost? Is anyone licensed and close to this neighborhood? Each of those becomes its own search, and the answer the homeowner reads is assembled from the businesses that satisfied several of them.

That is the whole shift in one paragraph. It is not that rankings stopped mattering. It is that there are now several rankings per question, and the answer is written by a system that is reading your pages and your profile the way a careful assistant would, not scanning for a keyword.

Why fan-out changes who gets found

Under the old model a business could own a search by being the best match for one phrase. Under fan-out, the business that gets named is the one that answers the most sub-questions clearly. That rewards a different kind of presence, and it punishes a few habits that used to be harmless.

It rewards specificity. A page that says "we offer plumbing services" answers nothing. A page that says the after-hours call-out fee, the neighborhoods covered, the average arrival time and the license number answers four sub-questions at once. The AI is looking for facts it can quote, and a business that states its facts gets quoted.

It rewards consistency. When one sub-search returns your Google Business Profile, another returns your website and a third returns a directory listing, the AI compares them. If your hours, phone and address disagree, you look unreliable to a system that is trying to recommend someone reliable. This is the same reason citation consistency has always mattered for the map pack, now with a second judge reading the evidence.

It rewards reviews that say something. "Great service" is noise. "They answered at 10pm and were here by 11" is an answer to a sub-question. We cover how to ask for reviews that say things in our guide to getting more Google reviews, and the short version is that the question you ask shapes the review you get.

It punishes thin pages. A location page that is a template with the city name swapped used to rank passably. In a fan-out world it answers none of the sub-questions that are specific to that place, so the AI reaches for a competitor who wrote something real. Multi-location brands feel this first; our franchise SEO page explains the fix.

Try it: the fan-out simulator

Type the kind of search a customer would use for your business. The simulator shows the sub-searches an AI is likely to run and which part of your presence answers each one. It is a model of the pattern, not a copy of Google's system, and it runs entirely in your browser.

Tags show where each sub-question is normally answered: Profile = Google Business Profile, Site = a page on your website, Reviews = what customers wrote.

Run it for your own category and notice how many of the sub-searches point at your profile and your reviews rather than your homepage. That is the pattern across almost every local service: the website carries the facts, the profile carries the hours and the proximity, and the reviews carry the proof. A business that is strong on only one of the three is being named in some answers and left out of most.

The five things a local business should fix first

1. State your facts where a machine can find them

Hours, service area by neighborhood, pricing structure, licenses, what you do not do, how fast you respond. Put them on the page in plain sentences, and mark them up with structured data so there is no ambiguity. Our AI SEO system is built around exactly this: content written so that a retrieval system can lift a fact out of it and attribute it to you.

2. Make the Google Business Profile the most complete one in your pack

Several of the sub-searches resolve to the map pack and the profile behind it. Primary category, every service listed, a real description, holiday hours and fresh photos are not nice-to-haves; they are the answers. The profile grader on our management page scores yours in two minutes.

3. Fix every mismatch between your profile, your site and your listings

One phone number. One address format. One set of hours. When the sub-searches return different versions of you, the AI has to choose which to believe, and it may choose none. Run the free NAP checker on our free SEO tools page if you are not sure where you stand.

4. Ask for reviews that answer questions

Change the ask from "would you leave us a review?" to "would you mention how fast we got there and what we fixed?" Reviews that contain specifics are the evidence AI Mode quotes when it explains why it recommended you.

5. Write one page per real question

Every sub-search in the simulator is a page topic. "What does an after-hours call cost" is a page. "Which neighborhoods do you cover" is a page. Not thin pages, and not the same page ten times; one honest, specific page per question, with the answer in the first paragraph. That is how a site becomes quotable.

What does not change

It would be convenient to say AI Mode replaces SEO. It does not. The sub-searches are still searches. They are still run against Google's index, still influenced by the same relevance, authority and proximity signals, and still dominated by businesses with strong sites and strong profiles. A business that is invisible in classic search is invisible in every one of the hidden searches too. Google's own explanation of how AI features in Search work makes the point directly: the foundation is still being crawlable, indexable and helpful.

What changes is the ceiling. Classic SEO got you a blue link. Fan-out can get you named in an answer the customer reads instead of scrolling, and that answer usually names two or three businesses, not ten. The reward for being specific, consistent and well reviewed is larger than it has ever been, and so is the cost of being generic.

A 30-day plan

  • Week 1: Run the simulator for your three most valuable searches. List every sub-search. Mark which ones your profile, site or reviews already answer and which are blank.
  • Week 2: Finish the profile: category, services, description, hours, photos. Correct every listing that disagrees with it.
  • Week 3: Write the two pages that answer the biggest blanks, each with a direct answer in the first paragraph and structured data behind it.
  • Week 4: Change the review ask. Send it to every customer from the last 60 days with the specific question you want answered.

Then measure. Search your category in AI Mode and in a regular search from a few points around your service area, and note who gets named. Repeat monthly. The businesses that get named consistently are the ones that treated this as a standing practice, which is what our local SEO and Google Maps program is.

Where the map pack fits in

The map pack did not go anywhere. For most local searches it is still the first thing on the page, and several of the sub-searches AI Mode runs are, in effect, map searches. A business in the top three of its pack is already in the evidence set the AI reads. A business outside it is competing to be found by a search that never shows the map. How fan-out plays out in six local industries

The pattern is the same everywhere, but the sub-questions are not. Here is what the hidden searches tend to look like for the industries we work in most, and the one asset that decides each.

Dentists. "Dentist near me accepting new patients," "same-day emergency dentist," "does this office take my insurance," "dentist reviews about pain or anxiety." The deciding asset is the profile plus a services page that names insurance carriers and emergency availability in plain words. Dental offices lose here when the insurance list lives only in a PDF. Our dental SEO page shows the full structure.

Roofers. "Roofer near me free inspection," "storm damage roof repair this week," "roofing company reviews insurance claim help," "roof replacement cost by square." The deciding asset is reviews that mention the insurance process, and a page that explains it. Roofers who write "we work with all insurance companies" and nothing else answer none of the sub-questions.

Restaurants. "Quiet restaurant for a first date under $40," "open late near me," "gluten-free options reviews," "reservations tonight." The deciding asset is the menu as text on the website, not an image, plus hours and the reservation link on the profile. Our restaurant network at restaurantsseo.com covers the three roads a diner takes to your door.

Law firms. "Personal injury lawyer free consultation," "how long does a case take," "attorney reviews about communication," "no fee unless we win near me." The deciding asset is attorney bio pages with real case types, and reviews that mention being kept informed. Firms with a single "practice areas" page are invisible to most of the fan-out.

HVAC. "AC repair today," "furnace replacement cost," "HVAC company financing," "technician reviews showed up on time." The deciding asset is same-day availability stated on the profile and a page with real price ranges. We built the industry's tools into our HVAC SEO page for exactly this reason.

Med spas. "Botox near me price per unit," "med spa reviews natural results," "nurse injector credentials," "consultation same week." The deciding asset is a services page with a real price structure and provider credentials on the page, not in a brochure. Med spas that hide pricing lose the price sub-search to whoever publishes it.

Common mistakes we see when businesses try to "optimize for AI"

Stuffing "AI" into the copy. Nobody searches for an "AI-optimized plumber." Write for the customer's question; the system is reading for answers, not keywords.

Publishing one giant FAQ page. Forty questions on one page dilutes every one of them. One real page per real question, each with a direct answer at the top, is what gets lifted into an answer.

Blocking AI crawlers. Some sites blocked GPTBot and similar crawlers in 2024 out of caution and never revisited it. A business that wants to be recommended by an assistant has to let the assistant read it. Our free robots.txt generator writes one that welcomes the crawlers that matter.

Treating the profile as finished. A Google Business Profile completed in 2021 is not complete in 2026. Services, attributes and categories have changed. The fastest wins in AI answers for local businesses almost always come from the profile.

Faking specifics. An AI that quotes your "same-day service" to a customer who then waits three days is a reputational problem you created. State what is true. The system rewards specificity because specificity is useful; it does not reward specificity that is wrong.

Ignoring the second-city problem. A business that serves two metros from one address answers the proximity sub-search in only one of them. Our guide to expanding to a second city covers the honest ways to fix that.

How to measure whether it is working

You cannot track AI Mode rankings in a rank tracker, because there are no rankings in the old sense. What you can do, once a month, is this:

  • Run your five most valuable searches in AI Mode and in a regular Google search, from three or four points across your service area. A phone on cellular data in each neighborhood is the honest way to do it.
  • Record which businesses are named in the AI answer and in what order. Record whether you are named, and what the answer says about you.
  • Count calls, direction requests and website clicks from your Google Business Profile. Those move first.
  • Watch Search Console for queries that look like sub-questions: longer, more specific, often phrased as a question. Growth there is the fingerprint of fan-out working for you.

Keep the notes in one place and compare month to month. Three months is enough to see a trend; six months is enough to be sure. If you would rather have the measurement done for you, it is a standard part of the monthly report in our SEO campaigns.

Questions readers ask

What is query fan-out in Google AI Mode?

Query fan-out is the technique Google describes for AI Mode and AI Overviews: a single question is split into several related searches that run at the same time, and the results of all of them are used to write one answer. It means a business is evaluated across several searches at once rather than ranked for one phrase.

Does my local business need to do anything differently for AI Mode?

Yes, but it is the same work done more thoroughly: state your facts plainly on your site, finish your Google Business Profile, make every listing agree, and collect reviews that mention specifics. Businesses that are invisible in classic search are invisible in AI Mode too.

Can I see which sub-searches Google runs for my category?

Not directly. Google does not publish the sub-queries. The simulator in this article models the pattern from how AI Mode answers are structured, and the most reliable check is to run your category in AI Mode and read which businesses are named and why.

Does the map pack still matter in AI Mode?

Yes. Several of the hidden searches resolve to local results, and businesses in the top three of their map pack are already in the evidence an AI reads. Google Business Profile strength is one of the most direct ways to be included in AI answers for local searches.

How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

Profile and consistency fixes can change what AI Mode says within weeks because Google reads profile changes quickly. New pages and reviews compound over the following months. Measuring monthly from a few points in your service area is the only way to know.
