The Complete Local SEO Checklist: Eight Weeks to the Map Pack

What local SEO is, in one paragraph

Local SEO is the set of things that decide whether your business shows up when someone nearby searches for what you sell. It has three surfaces now: the Google Maps pack, the organic results under it, and the answer an AI assistant gives when someone asks it instead of Google. Most guides cover the first. This checklist covers all three, in the order we actually do the work.

How to use this: work top to bottom. Each section is a week of part-time effort for a small business, or one call to us. Do not skip to the interesting parts; the boring foundations are the ones competitors skip, which is why they work.

Week 1: the foundation audit

  • Search your business name. Note every listing, duplicate and wrong detail.
  • Write down the exact name, address and phone you will use everywhere.
  • Load your site on a phone over cellular. Time it. Over three seconds is a problem.
  • Run your homepage through PageSpeed Insights and save the report.
  • Check your site for a canonical tag, a sitemap and a robots.txt that does not block anything important. Our free SEO tools help here.
  • Search your three main services in an incognito window and screenshot who ranks.

Week 2: the Google Business Profile

This is the single highest-return week. Fix the primary category, list every real service with a price range, write a plain description that names your towns, upload ten real photos, and build the review ask into every job. The full walkthrough is our Google Business Profile optimization guide.

  • Primary category matches the market leaders
  • Services listed with prices
  • Ten real photos uploaded, named before upload
  • Review link ready to hand to every customer
  • Every existing review answered

Week 3: the website, one page per service and town

Google cannot rank you for a service you do not have a page for. A single services page listing twelve things ranks for none of them well. Build one real page per service that gets searched, and one per town that has real demand, each with things only true of that place. Our service-area pages are built on that rule; if you can swap two town names and nothing becomes false, the pages are duplicates and will hurt you.

On every page: a title that leads with the service and town, one H1, an answer to the main question in the first paragraph, a phone number that is tappable, and a short form. Put your name, address and phone in the footer exactly as they appear on the profile.

Week 4: schema, the part the machines read

Structured data is a block of JSON in your page that states in machine terms who you are: business type, name, address, phone, hours, service area, the same details as your profile. Google uses it to connect your site to your listing. AI assistants use it to decide you are a real entity. Use the LocalBusiness type only with a real address; a service-area business without a storefront uses Organization with areaServed. Never invent an address to look local.

Week 5: citations and consistency

Citations are mentions of your name, address and phone on other sites: directories, the chamber, industry associations, the BBB. They matter less than they did a decade ago and they still matter, mostly for consistency. Claim the major ones, make every detail identical to the profile, and stop. Fifty low-quality directories do nothing.

Week 6: reviews as a system, not a favor

Decide who asks, when and how. The tech at close-out. The front desk at checkout. A text that evening. Ask customers to name the service and the town. Reply to every review within a day. That is the whole system, and it beats any review-generation software because the software cannot make the customer happy first.

Week 7: AI answers

People now ask assistants who to call. The assistant picks from pages that answer plainly. Under a question-shaped heading, give a direct answer in two or three sentences, then go deeper. State prices, service areas and facts a reader could check. Keep them identical across your site and profile. Our AI SEO service page explains the mechanics, and our case study shows what it looks like when an assistant names you.

Week 8: conversion, because ranking is not the goal

A page that ranks and does not produce calls is an expense. Put the phone number at the top and the bottom. Make the form three fields. Say what happens after they submit and how fast. Show proof where doubt shows up: reviews next to the price, photos next to the promise. Our conversion optimization page is the long version.

Monthly: the maintenance loop

  • Five new photos on the profile
  • One profile post
  • Answer every new review
  • Check calls and direction requests against last month
  • Re-run the three incognito searches and note movement
  • Ask an AI assistant your main question and note who it names

Local SEO is not a project with an end date. It is a set of habits. The businesses that hold the map pack for years are not doing anything exotic; they are doing this list every month while competitors do it once.

Questions readers ask

How long does local SEO take to work?

Profile changes show in weeks. Map pack movement often comes within two to four months. Organic rankings for service pages build over six to twelve. Nobody can promise a date.

Do I need a website to rank in the map pack?

Technically no, but in competitive markets the businesses at the top almost always have a site that supports the listing, and without one you cannot appear in organic or AI results at all.

How many citations do I need?

Claim the major directories and your industry's key ones with identical details, and stop. Consistency matters more than count.

Is local SEO different in a small town?

Less competition, fewer searches. One honest page and a strong profile often takes the market. Do not build pages for towns with no demand.

Can I do all of this myself?

Yes. Everything on this list is doable by an owner with a few hours a week. Most hire it out because the hours are the problem, not the difficulty.
