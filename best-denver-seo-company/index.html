Short answer

How do you tell if an SEO company is worth paying? Ask four things: whether the Google Analytics and Search Console accounts are in your name, who does the work day to day, what the link-building method actually is, and whether the monthly report shows calls and enquiries rather than impressions. An agency that answers all four plainly is usually fine. One that gets vague on any of them is the problem.

How long before SEO should show results? Foundations in the first three months, measurable ranking movement by month three to six, and a positive return somewhere between month six and twelve. Past a year with no change in the phone, get a second opinion.

Before You Hire Anyone

Three Things No Agency Can Fix For You

Worth sorting before you spend a dollar on search. A good agency will tell you about these on the first call. A weak one will happily bill around them.

🏷️ Your name and domain

Nobody can rank you well under a name people cannot spell, recall or find. Exact-match domains lost most of their ranking power years ago — but a memorable domain still drives direct traffic, referrals and click-through, and those are real.

📞 How you answer

The best campaign in Denver cannot survive a voicemail returned two days later. Most local search happens on a phone in the evening. Speed of response is free, entirely within your control, and frequently worth more than a ranking position — and with AI assistants now placing calls on a customer's behalf, an unanswered phone increasingly means the assistant simply moves to the next provider.

💬 What customers say

Reviews influence rankings, click-through and AI recommendations simultaneously. No agency can write them for you — that violates Google policy and FTC rules. The method is in our guide to getting more reviews.

🔍 Rebranding, or launching a second service line?

If you are naming something new — a spin-off brand, a second location, a service you want to market separately — get the domain settled before the marketing starts. Renaming later means rebuilding authority from scratch, and that is genuinely expensive.

The part the seven criteria cannot score

Do Not Hire an Agency for the Search Engine of 2019

The criteria above judge an agency on how it works today. This is the other question, and over a three-year engagement it is probably the more expensive one.

Search stopped being one box a while ago. A buyer now asks an assistant, reads an AI summary, checks a map, and only sometimes clicks a blue link. The businesses being named inside those answers are not necessarily the ones ranking first — they are the ones whose information is clear enough for a machine to repeat with confidence.

The next step is already visible. Assistants are moving from answering to acting — comparing options, checking availability, making the booking. When that lands properly, the thing being evaluated is not your homepage. It is whether your business is legible: consistent facts, real hours, a service area that matches reality, and a way to respond the moment something asks.

An agency still selling you keyword rankings in 2026 is not wrong exactly. It is just answering a question buyers have partly stopped asking.

We would rather be plain than dramatic about this: nobody knows the exact timeline, and anyone quoting you one is guessing. But the preparation is not speculative. Clean entity data, honest structured markup, fast pages, answer-shaped content and a real conversion path are the same work that wins ordinary search anyway. That is the part worth knowing — you are not gambling on a prediction. You are doing the fundamentals, and the fundamentals happen to be what the new surfaces reward.

It is also, honestly, the optimistic read. A small Denver business can now be found by a buyer who never typed its name, because a machine judged it the best answer. Twenty years ago that visibility had to be bought. Most of it is now earned by being genuinely clear about what you do and genuinely good at doing it. We think that is a better world to market in, not a worse one.

So the question to ask any agency you are considering, ours included: “Show me something you changed in the last twelve months because of how search actually works now.” A specific answer means they are paying attention. A vague one about staying on top of trends means you are funding someone's 2019 playbook for another year.
