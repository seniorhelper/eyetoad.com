The Cheapest Wins in Dentistry

Four Questions Patients Search That Most Practices Never Answer

These are not clever keywords. They are the four things a patient needs to know before booking, and a surprising number of practice websites answer none of them in text a search engine or an AI assistant can read.

"Does this dentist take my insurance?"

Put the plan names in text

Someone typing a specific plan name has already decided to book and is only filtering on who accepts them. If your accepted plans live in a PDF, an image, or a logo strip, you are invisible to that search — and to any assistant answering it on a patient's behalf.

"Are they accepting new patients?"

Say so, plainly and currently

It is a real search and a real hesitation. Stating it clearly on the site and on the Google Business Profile removes a reason to keep looking. Stating it and being wrong is worse than not stating it, so keep it current.

"How much does this actually cost?"

Give a range, not silence

Nobody expects a quote from a web page. But total silence on cost reads as evasive, and it sends the patient to a competitor who gave them a range. This is especially true for implants, Invisalign and cosmetic work.

"Can I get in today?"

Availability is an emergency ranking factor

For emergency searches, hours accuracy and same-day language matter more than anything on the page. A patient in pain is filtering on one thing: who can see me now.

What an AI Agent Actually Checks Before It Recommends a Dentist

None of it is exotic. It is the machine-readable version of what a careful patient would check — which is why it is winnable, and why complete data beats a large marketing budget here.

01

Does the practice resolve to one entity?

Name, address and phone identical across the web, including old directory listings. Conflicting details make you harder to trust and easier to skip.

02

Is the insurance list readable?

Plan names as text, not a PDF or a row of logos. An agent filtering for a specific plan cannot read an image.

03

Are services machine-readable?

Service schema and a page per procedure. "Comprehensive dental care" gives an agent nothing to match a query against.

04

Do reviews look current and real?

Recency and steady flow rather than a pile from 2021. Response rate is a signal too, and most practices never reply.

05

Is there a named, credentialled clinician?

A real dentist with a licence, a school and a bio. On a YMYL topic, anonymous content is a trust problem, not a neutral one.

06

Are hours and availability accurate?

Especially for emergency intent. Wrong hours do more damage than missing hours, because the patient shows up and you are closed.
