What we do

The Actual Work, Not the Category Names

Local SEO is unglamorous and largely a matter of doing every small thing properly. Here is the list.

Grid-mapped rank tracking

We measure your position from dozens of points across your service area, not just at your pin. That is how you find the neighborhoods where you are two spots away from the pack instead of forty — and those are the ones worth attacking first.

Profile rebuilt from the category up

Primary category chosen against what actually ranks in your market rather than what sounds right. Then every service listed with a real description, attributes, products, hours including holidays, and the Q&A section filled in — which you are allowed to seed yourself and almost nobody does.

A review engine, not a review request

A repeatable ask timed to the moment of highest satisfaction, made genuinely one tap. Reply templates for every rating including the bad ones. If you want the friction as close to zero as it gets, NFC tap cards put your review page on a customer's screen in about a second.

Citation and NAP cleanup

One canonical version of your name, address and phone, then corrected outward — Google first, then Apple and Bing, then aggregators, then the old listings from a previous address that are doing the real damage.

Service-area pages that are actually different

Real detail about each area: the neighborhoods, the housing stock, the jobs you have done there, different photos. Twelve pages identical except the city name is the exact thing Google's doorway-page policy describes, and it is the most common way local SEO goes wrong.

Tracking that survives zero-click

Call tracking and profile-action reporting, because a growing share of local customers now call you straight from the listing without ever loading your website. Judge that by sessions and you will conclude something is failing while the phone rings more.

If you go to the customer

No Storefront? The Rules Are Different.

Plumbers, roofers, mobile groomers, contractors, cleaners — anyone who works at the customer's address rather than their own. This is the part most guides skip, and it changes the strategy completely.

Proximity becomes a filter, not a score

For a service-area business, Google is mostly asking a yes-or-no question: does this searcher fall inside the area you declared? If yes, you are eligible. Position is then decided by relevance and prominence rather than distance.

That is good news and a trap at once. Good, because you are not permanently beaten by whoever has the closer office. A trap, because declaring a huge service area to cast a wider net weakens your relevance signals across all of it. Broad coverage has been costing businesses rankings rather than winning them.

  • Hide your address. An unhidden home address on a service-area profile is one of the fastest routes to suspension there is.
  • Never use a virtual office or a mailbox. Misrepresenting your location is the leading cause of profile suspension, and Google is good at detecting it.
  • Declare a realistic area. The places you genuinely serve, not the places you would accept a job from.
  • Match your site to your profile. If the declared areas and your website's locations disagree, Google cannot verify either.
  • Get reviews from across the territory. For a business with no fixed pin, reviews spread across your area are one of the few geographic signals you own.

Before someone talks you into it

Three Things That Can Cost You the Profile

These get sold as tactics. They are risks, and the downside is losing an asset you spent years building.

Keywords in your business name

You will see competitors doing it and ranking. Google prohibits it and enforces against it, and it is one of the two leading suspension triggers. Use the name on your signage and your paperwork.

Bought or incentivized reviews

Detectable, against platform policy, and a perfect five-star record with no substance reads as fake to human beings anyway. A 4.5 with real detail converts better than a flawless 5.0.

Extra listings for one location

Creating a second profile to cover another suburb does not double your visibility. It splits your signals and risks both listings.
