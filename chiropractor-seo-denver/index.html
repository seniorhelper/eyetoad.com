What is chiropractic marketing?

Chiropractic marketing is the work of getting a practice found and chosen by patients who do not already know you. It comes down to a handful of surfaces: the Google map pack that appears for chiropractor near me, the review profile a patient reads before booking, the website that has to turn that interest into an appointment, and the AI-generated answers that increasingly summarize local options before any list of links appears. Everything else — the photos, the intake forms, the recall texts — exists to support those.

The honest diagnosis

The three-mile problem

Inside your radius there are probably six to twelve other practices. Most of them bought their website from the same handful of industry template vendors. Same layout, same spine illustration, same paragraphs with the name swapped. A patient comparing three of you cannot tell you apart — so they stop trying.

What they do instead

They fall back on the two things that are easy to compare

Distance and reviews. Not technique, not years in practice, not the certifications on your wall — those require reading, and nobody is reading. Whichever pin is close enough and looks most recently alive gets the tap. This is why a newer practice with forty fresh reviews routinely beats a twenty-year practice with eleven old ones.

Where it actually costs you

You never see the patient you lost

There is no bounce report for someone who saw three practices and picked another one. The loss is invisible, which is why most owners conclude marketing does not work rather than that their listing was fourth. You feel it eventually, as a schedule with holes in it and no obvious explanation.

If you have ever wondered why a competitor outranks you when your care is plainly better, that is the answer in one sentence: the things a patient can compare in forty seconds are not the things you are best at.

Two patients, one search box

The person in pain and the person doing research are not the same customer

Building the practice site for only one of them is the most common structural mistake we see.

Acute

"Woke up and can't turn my head"

This person is uncomfortable right now and wants the soonest appointment they can get. They decide in under a minute from the map pack, and the deciding factors are whether you appear open, whether booking takes one tap, and whether someone answers. Long copy about your philosophy of care does nothing here. A hidden phone number loses them outright.

Considered

"Is a chiropractor covered by my insurance"

Ongoing discomfort, a referral from a friend, or an injury they have lived with for months. This person reads. They want to know what a first visit involves, what it costs, whether you take their plan, and whether you sound like someone they would trust with their spine. They may take four days. You win them with clarity and lose them to vagueness.

A practice built only for the first patient never fills its higher-value slots. Built only for the second, the phone never rings today. You need both, on separate pages, doing separate jobs.

The cheapest patients you will ever get

You are probably sitting on a year of bookings already

Most practices chase strangers while an old list goes cold in the practice-management software. The people on it already trust you, already know where you are, and already have a file. Almost none of them stopped because they were unhappy.

Step one

Pull the list nobody looks at

Patients with no visit in six to eighteen months who never formally discharged. In a mature practice that is usually hundreds of names, not dozens.

Step two

Say something human

Not a promotion. A short message that acknowledges it has been a while and makes rebooking take one tap. The tone does more work here than the offer does.

Step three

Make the return frictionless

No re-registration, no paperwork they already filled out, no phone tag. Every extra step reintroduces the reason they drifted off in the first place.

Step four

Then keep it running

A standing recall rhythm rather than an annual blast, so this never rebuilds into a backlog again. This is the part practices skip, and it is the part that compounds.

This is why we usually start here rather than with acquisition. It books appointments in weeks instead of months, it pays for the slower work, and it stops you paying to pour new patients into a practice that leaks the ones it has. Acquisition without retention is just a more expensive way to stay the same size — which is generally the real constraint, not traffic.

Local demand

The Denver demand a generic chiropractic playbook misses

National advice is written for an average city. Four sources of search volume here are specific to the Front Range, and each is a page most practices have not built.

Ski and trail season

Weekend warriors coming back off the passes generate a predictable seasonal wave of back, neck and shoulder searches. It arrives on the same rough calendar every year, which means it can be ranked for in advance instead of chased after. More on that pattern in Denver's seasonal search demand.

Desk-bound downtown

A dense professional core with a commuting population searching on a lunch break for something near the office rather than near home. That is a different radius, a different set of hours and often a different page than your neighborhood traffic.

Auto injury overlap

Collision patients search within days, often while also looking for representation, and the language has nothing in common with wellness care. It deserves its own page — and it sits right next to the work we do in personal injury.

Metro fragmentation

Nobody crosses town for an adjustment. Denver is a dozen separate local contests, and winning your own few square miles beats ranking vaguely everywhere. We wrote about why in how the metro splits up.
